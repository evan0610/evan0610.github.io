About Me

I am Tianchun (Evan) Wu, an Electronic Engineering undergraduate at The Chinese University of Hong Kong. My research lies at the intersection of embodied AI, medical robotics, and robot learning, with a particular focus on intelligent robotic endoscopy.

At the Robotics, Perception and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (RPAI Lab), I work on learning-based robotic systems that integrate multimodal perception, language reasoning, and physical control. I currently lead and contribute to research projects spanning autonomous endoscopic navigation, vision-language-action learning, and intelligent endoscopic intervention.

My recent research has increasingly focused on self-evolving embodied intelligence: enabling robotic systems to acquire, evaluate, and improve task skills through interaction and experience rather than remaining limited to a fixed policy or a single predefined task.

My long-term research goal is to develop generalizable and adaptive robotic intelligence for real-world medical environments, particularly systems that can continuously improve while operating safely alongside clinicians. I plan to continue toward research-based postgraduate study in robotics and embodied intelligence.

Selected Research Projects

EndoNav

Self-Evolving Autonomous Endoscopic Navigation

A research platform for autonomous endoscopic navigation that investigates how embodied robotic agents can learn, evaluate, and progressively improve navigation skills through interaction with physical environments.

The project combines multimodal perception, robot-state information, vision-language reasoning, and action generation for closed-loop endoscope control. Recent work explores self-evolving robotic agents that construct reusable skills from demonstrations and interaction experience, enabling adaptation beyond fixed task-specific policies.

My work spans system design, learning architecture development, dataset collection, physical robot experimentation, baseline evaluation, and autonomous navigation studies across different anatomical conditions.

ColonVLA

Vision-Language-Action Learning for Robotic Endoscopy

A vision-language-action framework for intelligent robotic endoscopy, connecting endoscopic visual observations, language-level task understanding, robot states, and continuous control actions.

The project investigates how multimodal foundation models can support generalizable endoscopic robot behavior across navigation and intervention tasks, with particular interest in transferring learned skills across environments and task settings.

My work focuses on multimodal dataset construction, VLM/VLA training and evaluation, action representation, robot integration, and physical validation.

EndoRinse

Adaptive Robotic Endoscopic Rinsing

An intelligent robotic endoscopy system for autonomous rinsing and scene recovery under visually degraded endoscopic conditions.

The project studies how an embodied robotic agent can perceive contamination, reason about cleaning requirements, execute coordinated rinsing actions, and evaluate task outcomes in a closed loop. Ongoing work explores self-improving task policies that can accumulate reusable rinsing skills from physical interaction and experience.

My work covers robotic system integration, VLA policy design, embedded control, task-level autonomy, and phantom-based physical validation.

EndoVLA-OOV

Open-Vocabulary Vision-Language-Action for Robotic Endoscopy

A robotic endoscopy project exploring open-vocabulary perception, visual-language reasoning, and action-conditioned model behavior under endoscopic scenes.

My work focuses on VLM/VLA baseline evaluation, domain-specific data preparation, and model tuning for endoscopic robotic tasks.

Open-H Dataset

Healthcare Robotics Dataset Collaboration with NVIDIA

Contributed high-quality manually collected endoscopic robotic data for the OpenHDS collaboration with NVIDIA, supporting healthcare robotics and surgical robot learning research.

The work connects domain-specific endoscopic data collection with large-scale robotics datasets and robot policy training.

Exploring Continuum Robot Agents for Fire Rescuing in Buildings

Continuum Robotics and Agentic Autonomy

A continuum robotics project for navigation and rescue assistance in structurally constrained building-fire scenarios.
